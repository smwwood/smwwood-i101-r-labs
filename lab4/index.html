Part 3 · 16 min

Turning words into data

Now we'll make new logical variables that reveal whether particular words appear in descriptions.

Our detective strategy

Choose a word, search every classroom-approved complaint description, save the TRUE/FALSE result in a new column, then count the TRUE values.

A new column doesn't replace the old description: it adds information to the same dataset.

Part 4 · 24 min

Two datasets are better than one

What happens when scooter activity and resident complaints join forces?

Our research question

Do months with more scooter trips also tend to have more complaints? And does scooter presence tell a different story?

Activity dataset

Multiple providers and dates, with trip totals and available scooters.

Complaints dataset

One row per report, with its date and text.

We cannot simply join those original rows: they don't represent the same thing. We first summarize each table to one row per year-month, then join on the shared year_month value.

The join keeps months with recorded scooter activity. Months with no matching complaint record are set to zero recorded complaints. This assumes the complaints extract is complete for those months.
